The product

Six specialists. One decision, seen from every side.

Each answers a question residents and councils actually ask, from the same documents, checked the same way.

Decision briefs

What's proposed and why now, how it may affect you, and when you can have a say. Pick who you are (renter, transit rider, senior, newcomer, business owner) and see cited impacts with honest "may" language. Ask anything, in any language, and get an answer with numbered citations.

Transit access gaps

Connection Scan over the region's bus and light-rail schedules finds the hospitals, groceries and schools a neighbourhood can't reach in 45 minutes, then ranks fixes by people affected.

Lifecycle costs

The upfront price next to 25 to 40 years of operating, maintenance, renewal and environmental costs, per household. Every input is cited or a slider residents can move.

Commitments tracker

The public promises a decision touches, from climate targets to housing pledges: who made each one, the target, the latest progress with its source, and what's still unclear.

Housing pipeline

Units from approval to permit to construction, from open permit data, with the time between stages where the data allow, and where the public trail goes dark.

Construction scout Illustrative

For a project with a line, agents scout candidate work sites from OpenStreetMap, score them by stated weights (room, distance from schools, homes and hospitals, truck access) and stage the work with what residents would notice at each step. The demo is an illustrative scenario, not a Region plan.

Why cities can trust it

A city can't publish what it hasn't checked. So we check everything.

  • Every fact has its page

    Click any figure to open the source page with the quote highlighted. If a quote can't be found on the page it cites, the statement is never shown as verified.

  • Nothing is official until staff approve it

    Agents draft. A named clerk or communications officer approves, edits or rejects each statement, then publishes under their own name. Until then, the brief says plainly that it's an unreviewed draft, as every demo brief does today.

  • A record that can't be edited quietly

    Every brief, approval, question and answer is added to a hash-chained public record. Anyone can check the chain in their browser.

  • Have your say, on time

    A timeline of the decision with the next chance to speak, write in or answer a survey, plus the deadline, the contact and an add-to-calendar button.

  • Lenses on the same decision

    Transit access gaps, lifecycle cost per household, the city's own commitments, and housing from approval to construction, all computed from open data with the method shown.

  • Plain words, for everyone

    Briefs aim for a grade 8 reading level, measured and shown. Designed for WCAG 2.2 AA. Read it in 11 languages; machine translations are labelled.

Where it started

Born at CIVIC Hack 2026.

Municipal Agents began at CIVIC Hack 2026, run by the Future Cities Institute at the University of Waterloo, 12 to 14 November 2026. The challenge we chose asked:

“How might we help residents understand a proposed municipal decision, how it could affect them, and when they can have a say?”

Challenge 1: Making municipal decisions easier to understand

We answered all three parts, then built the other four challenges in as lenses on the same decision, so a resident never has to leave the brief to see the whole picture.

Questions

Asked and answered.

Something else? Get in touch.

Who uses it today?

No municipality yet. Municipal Agents is a working prototype built at CIVIC Hack 2026 from public Waterloo Region documents and open data. We're looking for our first pilot municipalities.

Is this the city's official notice?

No. Every page says so and links to the official notice, which always prevails. Municipal Agents explains a decision; it doesn't replace legal notice or the city's consultation.

Can the AI get things wrong?

Yes, which is why it never gets the last word. Code, not a model, checks that every quote is on the page it cites. Sentences with figures that no checked statement supports are removed from answers. Nothing is official until a named staff member approves each statement.

Does it tell residents what to think?

No. The agents explain, lay out the trade-offs on both sides, and say what's unknown. They never decide and never recommend a position.

Who approves what residents see?

City staff. Agents produce a draft; a clerk or communications officer approves, edits or rejects each statement and publishes under their name. The approval is added to the public record. The demo briefs are unreviewed drafts, and each one says so at the top.

Do you collect personal information?

No, by design. Residents don't need an account, and there are no tracking scripts. Questions are logged with no identifier, and IP addresses are kept in memory only for rate limiting, never written. Please don't type personal details into a question, because questions are part of the public record.

Where is data stored, and where does the AI run?

The prototype stores its record in Toronto and uses a US-hosted AI model. Running the model in Canada or on the city's own infrastructure is planned for City and Region plans. Only public documents and residents' questions are sent to the model.

Is it accessible, and is it available in French?

It's designed to WCAG 2.2 AA, beyond Ontario's WCAG 2.0 AA requirement; an independent audit comes before the first paid launch. French is included in every plan, and machine translations are labelled.

How does a municipality start?

With a 90-day paid pilot on one live decision, priced below typical low-value purchase limits. We set up your city from your public documents, your staff review and publish, and you get a short report you can take to council. Book a pilot.

Is the code open?

Yes. The code is on GitHub, so a city's IT team can read exactly what runs.
