How we price

Four rules.

  • Residents never pay.No resident accounts, no ads, no data sales. Ever.
  • The price follows the population.That's how municipal budgets already work, and it tracks how many decisions and questions a city has.
  • Pilots fit a purchasing card.The pilot sits below typical Ontario low-value purchase limits, and a three-year City term sits below the CFTA open-tender threshold. Check your own purchasing by-law.
  • Hard caps on spend.Every agent run and every day has a dollar cap enforced in code. A city can't get a surprise bill.

The pilot

What 90 days proves.

On one live decision (a budget, a housing application, a transit change) we measure what a council will want to know before buying:

  • Staff review time per brief. Our estimate is 30 to 60 minutes. It hasn't been measured yet; the pilot measures it.
  • The reading grade of every published brief.
  • Citation failures caught before anything was published.
  • Resident use: views, questions asked, calendar adds.
  • Errors residents report, and how fast they were fixed.

You get a short written report you can take to council, whatever you decide next.

Why it's affordable

Model costs are small. People are the cost.

Our first full brief on the Region of Waterloo's budget, with four lenses, cost US$0.24 in model fees. An average resident question in our test suite cost about seven US cents. Reading a published brief costs nothing: it's served as stored, reviewed text.

So most of what you pay for is onboarding your sources, support and hosting, not tokens.
