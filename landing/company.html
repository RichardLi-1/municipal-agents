Company

Everyone affected by a decision should be able to understand it.

Our mission is to help residents understand the decisions that shape their city, how those decisions may affect them, and when they can have a say, and to help the people who serve them explain it well.

The story

A 191-page budget book and a noon deadline.

The Region of Waterloo's 2027 budget will set regional property taxes and water rates for everyone in its three cities and four townships. The facts are public, but they're spread across a 191-page budget book, asset management reports, transit plans and engagement pages: 344 pages in 19 PDFs and 4 web pages. To speak at council, a resident has to register by noon on 9 December. Most people it affects will never find that date.

City staff have the opposite problem. They have to explain decisions accurately, to everyone, in many languages, with small teams. A general chatbot doesn't help: it can state a wrong figure with confidence, and a city can't publish what it hasn't checked.

We started Municipal Agents at CIVIC Hack 2026, run by the Future Cities Institute at the University of Waterloo from 12 to 14 November 2026, to close that gap. AI does the reading and drafting. Code checks every figure against its page. Staff decide what's published. Residents get five minutes instead of three hundred pages.

The prototype is live for three real Waterloo Region decisions, built from public data only: the 2027 Plan and Budget, the Water Supply Strategy, and the zoning variances for Kitchener's new central transit hub.

How we build

Seven rules, enforced in code.

These aren't values on a wall. Each one is a check the product runs or a line it won't cross.

  1. Every figure cites a page.A statement is accepted only if each quote is found on that page of that source. Unverified statements are shown as such, never silently.
  2. Plain words.Grade 8 or lower, measured and shown.
  3. Agents never decide.They explain, lay out trade-offs on both sides, and say what's unknown. They never say what residents should think.
  4. Staff publish.Nothing is official until a named staff member approves it. Until then a brief says it's a draft, as the demo briefs do.
  5. The record can't be edited quietly.Every brief, check, question, answer, approval and dollar spent is appended to a hash-chained log anyone can verify.
  6. Not the official notice.Every page says where the official notice is.
  7. Public data only.No personal information. Questions aren't tied to anyone, and residents never need an account.
