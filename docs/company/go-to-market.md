# Municipal Agents: go-to-market

This is a plan as of 14 November 2026. We have no customers yet. The thresholds and
programs below come from public sources read on 14 November 2026. Every municipality has
its own purchasing by-law, and it decides how that municipality buys. Read it before any
sales call.

## 1. How an Ontario municipality buys software

### 1.1 Its own purchasing by-law

- The *Municipal Act, 2001* requires every municipality to adopt and maintain a
  procurement policy (s. 270, as summarized by
  [opencouncil.ca, 2024](https://opencouncil.ca/municipal-procurement-ontario/)).
  Methods and dollar limits vary by municipality.
- **A typical ladder** ([opencouncil.ca](https://opencouncil.ca/municipal-procurement-ontario/),
  edited 3 Jul 2025):
  - low-dollar-value purchases up to about $15,000
  - informal request for quotation (RFQ) from about $10,000 to $50,000
  - RFQ from about $50,000 to $100,000
  - request for tender above $100,000
  - requests for proposal (RFPs) used when price is not the only factor
- **Examples:**
  - Town of Essex: informal RFQ or RFP from $15,001 to $50,000, and formal from $50,001 to
    $120,000. Council approval is required above $50,000 when the lowest compliant bid
    is not chosen
    ([Essex by-law 2129 summary](https://essex-ca.govstack.com/media/eiwhjvqe/summary-of-procurement-by-law-2129.pdf)).
  - City of Thorold: the CAO can approve up to $25,000; council approves above that
    ([Thorold Policy 300-01, 29 Apr 2025](https://www.thorold.ca/media/5z1iw20i/policy_300-01-april-29-2025.pdf)).
- **What this means for us:** a CA$7,500 pilot is a low-value purchase in most by-laws.
  A three-year Town contract ($36,000) usually needs an informal quote. A three-year City
  contract ($108,000) usually needs a formal RFQ or RFP under the municipality's own rules.
  Many by-laws also allow single-source purchases in defined cases. We should never
  count on that, and should be ready to compete.

### 1.2 Trade agreements (2026 to 2027)

| Agreement | Municipal (MASH) threshold: goods / services | Construction | Source |
|---|---|---|---|

- Contract value includes renewals. Nova Scotia's guide says to count "the value of the
  Initial Contract period PLUS the estimated value of the renewal periods" (same table).
- **What this means for us:** Pilot, Town and City contracts stay below the CFTA
  threshold. A three-year Region contract (about $288,000 or more) goes above it, so it
  needs an open competitive process or a cooperative agreement that already complies.

### 1.4 Innovation and pilot routes

- **FCI CIVIC (City Innovation Via Internships and Co-op).** The Future Cities Institute
  at the University of Waterloo launched CIVIC in 2025. It pairs municipal partners with
  Waterloo students, treats each co-op term as a research project, and packages
  solutions so other municipalities can adopt them. The City of Edmonton was an early
  partner ([UWaterloo News, 10 Sep 2026](https://uwaterloo.ca/news/students-driving-change-canadian-cities-and-communities)).
  **Our use:** a city takes a CIVIC co-op student to run the pilot's evaluation (review
  time, comprehension testing). That gives the city capacity and gives us independent
  measurement. FCI also runs CIVIC Hack, whose challenges were voted on at the 2026 State
  of Canada's Cities Summit
  ([event page](https://uwaterloo.ca/engineering/events/civic-hackathon-2026)).
- **FCI i-Capstone.** Teams of UWaterloo students from several disciplines work for one
  or two terms on a municipality's challenge "at no cost to the local government"
  ([FCI, 17 Aug 2026](https://uwaterloo.ca/future-cities-institute/news/practical-tools-local-leaders-fci-amo-2026)).
  This suits smaller municipalities that want a supported evaluation before they buy a
  Town plan.
- **Innovative Solutions Canada (ISC).** ISC buys and tests Canadian innovations for
  *federal* departments, not municipalities. One open challenge sits right next to what
  we do: Shared Services Canada's "AI decision-making process visibility, traceability
  and compliance governance". It accepts Phase 2 proposals only, up to $2,000,000 per
  contract, and closes 22 Dec 2026, 14:00 ET
  ([ISED](https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/innovative-solutions-canada/en/node/281)).
  Our citation verifier and hash-chained record match its traceability and auditability
  goals. We would need a federal use case and a Canadian corporation to bid; decide by
  1 Dec 2026. The ISC Testing Stream is a later option for a federal "plain-language
  explanation of a decision" use case
  ([TS12 notice](https://canadabuys.canada.ca/en/tender-opportunities/tender-notice/cb-451-4570525)).
- **MaRS Discovery District and municipal innovation offices.** These are possible
  sources of introductions and pilot programs. We have not confirmed any specific
  program terms. Ask FCI and AMO which Ontario municipalities run formal innovation
  pilots.

## 2. First 10 customers

### 2.1 Ideal first customer

- A lower-tier or single-tier Ontario municipality of **50,000 to 250,000** people. These
  have enough decisions to justify a subscription, and procurement is still light.
- It has a **provincial housing target**. 50 municipalities have them
  ([Ontario](https://www.ontario.ca/page/tracking-housing-supply-progress)): 29 large and
  fast-growing ones assigned in Oct 2022 ([ERO 019-6171](https://ero.ontario.ca/notice/019-6171))
  and 21 more in 2023 ([ERO 019-7281](https://ero.ontario.ca/notice/019-7281)). Housing
  decisions such as official plan amendments, zoning, development charges and
  infrastructure are frequent, contested and time-limited.
- It already uses an **engagement platform** (EngagementHQ hubs are common; see
  [opencouncil.ca](https://opencouncil.ca/municipal-public-engagement/)) and an
  **agenda system** (eSCRIBE or CivicWeb). The budget line exists and the documents are
  digital.
- A transit agency publishes a **GTFS** feed, so the transit lens works on day one.
- It has a communications team of at least two people, and a clerk who will act as
  reviewer.

### 2.4 Sales motion

- **Founder-led.** We start with the communications director or the clerk and bring in
  IT and privacy in the first week with the [trust package](trust-and-compliance.md).
  Most deals are lost at the IT and privacy stage, so they should hear from us early.
- **The demo is their own decision.** A private preview built from their public PDFs in
  under a day, with every quote clickable.
- **Pilot success criteria are written up front**: review minutes per brief, reading
  grade, citation errors caught before publication, resident views and questions, and
  calendar adds.

### 2.5 Sales cycle (estimates)

| Step | Typical time | Why |
|---|---|---|
| First meeting to signed pilot | 4 to 8 weeks | Below the low-value threshold, but IT and privacy still review it |
| Pilot | 90 days | One decision from staff report to council vote |
| Pilot to annual contract | 3 to 9 months | New spending usually has to be in the next operating budget, and most Ontario municipalities adopt budgets between November and March |
| First meeting to first annual revenue | **6 to 12 months** | Plan cash accordingly; pilot revenue covers onboarding cost only |

## 5. What we will not do

- Publish a brief about a municipality's decision as if it were official, or without
  "not the official notice" and a link to the notice.
- Replace a city's legal notice, consultation process or engagement platform.
- Sell resident data. We collect no personal data by design ([architecture §6](../architecture.md)).
