---
title: Guide for residents
description: How to find a decision near you, read its brief, check any figure on its source page, ask a question, read it in your language and take part.
---

Municipal Agents explains decisions your local governments are about to make, in plain
words. Each decision has a **brief**: what is proposed, how it may affect you, and when
you can have a say. AI agents draft the brief from the city's own public documents, and
city staff check every statement before it is published. Every figure links to the page
it came from, so you never have to take our word for it.

You don't need an account. Nothing you do on the site is tied to you.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> A brief is not the official notice. Every brief has a **Read the official notice**
> link to the city's own page. If the two ever disagree, the official notice is right.

## Find a decision

The decisions page shows **What's being decided near you**: a map of Waterloo Region
with the area each decision touches, and a list of decision cards.

Each card shows:

- a plain title, for example "The Region's 2027 budget: what your regional taxes and
  water bills will pay for next year";
- who decides it ("Decided by Regional Council, Region of Waterloo");
- the **Next chance to have a say**, with a countdown in days;
- whether the brief is **Approved by staff** or still a **Draft**;
- which lenses it has (Transit, Costs, Commitments, Housing).

The soonest chance to take part comes first.

### Search by neighbourhood or street

Type in **Find your neighbourhood or street**, for example "Fairview", "King Street"
or "Hespeler". Pick a place from the list (arrow keys and Enter work). The list splits
into decisions **Near** that place (within 5 km) and **Elsewhere in the region**.

The list of places is built into the page, so what you type never leaves your browser.

## Read a brief

Open a decision to see its brief. The top of the brief always shows:

- the plain title, and the official title under it;
- the review status:
  - **Approved by (name) on (date)**: a named staff member reviewed every statement
    and published the brief;
  - **In review by city staff**: staff have started checking it;
  - **Draft: not yet reviewed by city staff**: the agents wrote it and nobody at the
    city has checked it yet. Read it with that in mind.
- the reading level, for example **Grade 7 reading level**. We aim for grade 8 or
  lower and show the real number, measured by code (the Flesch-Kincaid grade), even
  when it is higher;
- **Not the official notice.** and a link to the official one;
- the next chance to have a say, with a countdown.

The brief is split into tabs. Use the mouse, or the arrow keys once a tab has focus.
A decision only shows the tabs it has content for.

| Tab | What you'll find |
|---|---|
| **Overview** | **What's proposed** (3 to 6 short statements), **Key figures**, **Why now**, and **Words to know** (a short glossary). |
| **Affects you** | Pick who you are to see how the decision may affect you. |
| **Have your say** | The timeline, the next chance to take part, how to take part, and **Add to calendar**. |
| **Transit** | Who can reach everyday places by bus and ION, where the gaps are, and possible fixes. |
| **Costs** | The upfront price next to the costs over the asset's whole life, and per household. |
| **Commitments** | Public promises the decision touches (for example a climate target) and whether they're on track. |
| **Housing** | Homes from approval to move-in, and where progress gets hard to track. |
| **Construction** | An illustrative construction scenario, step by step, when a decision has one. It is always labelled as a scenario, not a plan. |
| **Unknowns & trade-offs** | **What the documents don't answer** (why it matters and who can answer), and **Trade-offs and possible harms**. |
| **Sources** | Every document the agents read: publisher, date, pages, licence, **View page 1** and **Open original**. |

The lens tabs (Transit, Costs, Commitments, Housing, Construction) are explained in
the [guide for planners](planners.md).

### What the labels mean

Each statement can carry a small label:

| Label | Meaning |
|---|---|
| A tick (screen readers hear "Checked") | Code found the quoted words on the cited page. |
| **Not verified** | The quote was not found on the cited page. Treat the statement with care. |
| **Edited by staff** | A staff member reworded what the agents wrote. The original wording is kept in the record. |
| **Corrected by the Municipal Agents team (not Region staff)** | The team that runs this site fixed what the agents wrote (wording, a citation or the kind of statement). Every corrected quote was checked on its page again. It is not a staff review: the brief stays a draft until city staff approve it. |
| **Check: the quote may not fully support this** | The quote is on the page, but it shares few words or numbers with the sentence. Read the source before relying on it. |
| **Estimate**, **Assumption** | A number that is an estimate, or an assumption the documents make. |
| **Unknown** | Something the documents don't say. |
| **Trade-off**, **Possible harm** | What one side gains and the other may lose, or who could be harmed. |
| **Computed** | A number worked out by code from open data (for example travel times), not quoted from a document. Open it to see **How this is worked out**. |

Statements that staff rejected, or that the Municipal Agents team withdrew, are not shown.

## Check any figure

Every statement has one or more page chips, like **p. 12**. Every number in **Key
figures** is a button too.

Select one to open the **Source page**:

- the PDF page itself, with the quoted words highlighted;
- **Quote found and highlighted.**, or **Quote not found on this page.** if the
  highlight could not be placed;
- **Previous page** and **Next page** (or Alt plus the left and right arrow keys);
- **Open original** to open the full document where the city published it.

If the PDF can't be drawn, the viewer shows the page's text instead (**Showing the
page's text.**). Press Escape to close it.

A source page has its own link, `/source/<document>/<page>` (add `?q=` and a few words
to highlight them), so you can share the exact page with someone. For example,
[/source/row-2026-budget-book/12](https://municipal-agents.fly.dev/source/row-2026-budget-book/12).

> [!NOTE]
> A tick means the words are on that page. It does not prove the sentence reads them
> fairly. That judgement is what staff review is for, and why you can always open the
> page yourself.

## See how it affects you

On **Affects you**, answer **Who are you? Pick all that apply.** You can pick more
than one:

I rent · I own my home · I ride transit · I drive · I walk or cycle · I run a business ·
I'm a senior · I'm a student · I'm a parent · I have a disability · I'm new to Canada

Each choice shows the statements written for that group. Impacts always use "may",
"might" or "could", because they are not certain. If the documents say nothing about
a group, you'll see **Nothing in the documents about this group yet.**

Your choices are also sent with any question you ask, so the answer can focus on you.

## Have your say

**Have your say** lists every step of the decision in date order: reports, committee
meetings, public input, council and deadlines. Past steps are marked **Passed**. The
next one is marked **Next chance** and shows how long is left (to the meeting, or to
the registration deadline if there is one).

Under **How to take part** you'll find each way in, with its instructions:

- **Speak** (register as a delegation), **Write**, **Survey**, **Attend**, **Watch**
  or **Contact your councillor**;
- the **Deadline**, the **Contact** (public office contacts only) and a **More
  information** link.

The dates and ways to take part are checked by hand against the official pages; they
are not written by the agents.

### Add the dates to your calendar

**Add to calendar** downloads a calendar file (`.ics`) that works with Google
Calendar, Apple Calendar and Outlook. Open the file to add the dates. It holds:

- every upcoming step that has a way to take part, with a reminder the day before;
- a separate entry for each registration deadline that comes before a meeting;
- for something already open, like a survey, an entry on its last day ("Last day: ...");
- the link to the brief and to the official notice.

## Ask a question

The **Ask about this decision** panel sits beside the brief (on a phone, switch between
**Brief** and **Ask** at the top). Type in plain words, in any language, or pick one of
the suggested questions.

While the agents work, **Agents at work** shows each step:

1. The lead agent reads your question and makes a **Plan**.
2. It hands parts of the question to specialists (for example the explainer or the
   transit specialist). Each one shows as working, done or failed.
3. Under each specialist you can see the tools it used, such as `search_pages`,
   `read_page` or `record_statement`, with a one-line summary.

The answer comes with page chips, like the brief. Before you see it, code removes any
sentence that tells you how to vote or what to think, and any sentence with a number
that no checked statement supports.

Answers explain; they never tell you what to think. Check the cited pages.

### Saved answers

If someone has already asked the same question (same decision, language and choices
under **Affects you**), you may get that answer again, labelled **Answered by the
agents on (date) · replayed from the record**. It costs nothing and shows the same
checked citations.

### When the agents can't answer

- If the AI model can't be reached, you'll see **The agents can't be reached right
  now, so here is what the checked brief says.** The answer is then built by code from
  the brief's checked statements, without a model.
- If the documents don't cover something, the answer says so and names who can answer.

### Limits

To keep the service fair and affordable:

| Limit | What you'll see |
|---|---|
| Questions up to 1,000 characters | "Please keep questions under 1000 characters." |
| 30 new agent runs (questions or translations) per 10 minutes from one internet address; saved and offline answers don't count | "You've sent 30 questions in 10 minutes. Please wait ... and try again." |
| 4 agent runs at once across the site | "The agents are busy with other questions. Please try again in a minute." |
| A daily budget for the AI agents (US$5 on the public site) | "Today's budget for the AI agents (US$5.00) has been used. The briefs are still here to read; questions open again after midnight Toronto time." |

A question stops after 4 minutes, 40 model calls or US$0.50, whichever comes first.
Briefs, sources and the record keep working when any limit is reached.

> [!WARNING]
> Questions are part of the public record. Please don't type your name, address,
> phone number or anything else personal.

## Read it in your language

Use the language menu to pick a language. Waterloo Region's site offers English,
French, Punjabi, Arabic, Chinese, Spanish, Portuguese, German, Romanian,
Persian and Vietnamese.

- The **brief** is translated by AI agents the first time someone asks for that
  language. That can take a minute (**Translating...**). After that it is saved and
  shown at once, until staff change the English brief.
- A translated brief says **Machine translation by AI. The English brief is the reference.**
  Use **Show the original** to switch back to English at any time.
- Citations keep the original English quote, so you can check it on the page.
- Arabic and Persian read right to left.
- Buttons and labels are in English and French. In other languages the brief is
  translated and the labels stay in English.
- You can **ask** in any language; the answer comes back in the language you picked.

You can also add `?lang=fr` (or another code) to a link to open the site in that
language.

> [!NOTE]
> Translations are made by a machine and are not reviewed by staff. If something seems
> off, compare with the English original.

## The public record

**The record** (`/log`) lists everything that happened, in order: briefs started and
drafted, statements the checker accepted or rejected, staff reviews and publications,
questions and answers, translations and each model call's cost.

Each entry's fingerprint (a SHA-256 hash) covers the entry before it, so changing any
past entry would break every one after it. Your browser re-checks the whole chain
itself and shows **Chain intact: (n) entries · checked in your browser**, or **Chain
broken at entry (n)**. You don't have to trust the server's word.

You can filter by kind and by decision, search, and open **Show details** on any entry.
Questions in the record have no name, account or internet address attached.

Nothing is deleted from the record. When a city's records rules say an entry's content
must be destroyed (for example a question that included someone's address), staff can
purge that content: the entry stays in the list and in the chain check with its details
empty, and a later `record.purged` entry says which one, why and who did it.

## What the agents cost

**What the agents cost** (`/spend`) shows, for the whole site: US dollars spent, model
calls, tokens, an estimate of the energy used and the CO2e, how much of today's limit
is spent, and a breakdown by model. Energy and emissions are estimates worked out from
the number of tokens, not measurements.

## Your privacy

- No accounts, no sign-in and no tracking scripts.
- Your theme and language choices are kept in your own browser.
- The rate limit holds your internet address in the server's memory only, and never
  writes it down.
- Questions are public in the record, with no identity attached.
- The map tiles come from OpenFreeMap, straight from your browser.

More detail: [security and privacy](security-privacy.md).

## Accessibility

The site aims to meet WCAG 2.2 AA. An automated check (axe-core) found no violations on
the main pages and every decision tab, at desktop and phone widths, in light and dark
themes. It works with a keyboard and a screen reader
(there's a **Skip to content** link, the tabs follow the arrow keys, and answers are
announced when ready). It follows your system's light or dark theme, reduced motion and
higher contrast settings. You can switch the theme under **Settings**, **Appearance** (System, Light or Dark).

It has not yet been tested with residents who use assistive technology; that is
**Planned**. If something doesn't work for you, please tell the city clerk's office or
the team.
