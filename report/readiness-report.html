Municipal Agents

AI agents read a city's own documents and turn a proposed decision into a brief a resident can read in five minutes: what's proposed, how it affects you, and when you can have a say. Every figure links to its page; staff approve every statement.

The problem

Budgets, transit changes and rezonings are explained in long staff reports. Residents find out late and miss the chance to speak. Municipal Agents drafts a plain brief from the city's own documents. Code checks every quote on its page; staff approve every statement. Seven specialists, five lenses: transit, cost, commitments, housing, construction.

Case study: Region of Waterloo 2027 budget

Decision: the 2027 regional budget (property tax, water rates). Under strong-mayor rules, the Regional Chair proposes it by 1 Feb 2027; Council may amend, the Chair veto, Council override by two-thirds.8 Overview at Council 16 Dec (to speak, register by noon 9 Dec); survey open to 26 Feb 2027. The draft isn't public, so the brief uses last year's forecast.

Sources: 27 public documents, 412 pages.

Result: 30 statements, each quote found on its page; 11 figures; reading grade 7.4 (target 8); not yet staff-reviewed. Evals: 6 of 12 pass; 5 of 6 failures were reading grade only; no citation failures or voting advice.

Lesson: when the Gemini credit ran out (~02:20 ET), the site kept answering from recorded answers and checked statements. Production needs billing alerts.

How another municipality adapts it

  1. cities/<id>.json: map, languages, contacts, GTFS. ~1 h
  2. PDFs into data/sources; npm run sources. ~1 h
  3. data/decisions/<id>.json, clerk-checked. ~2–4 h each
  4. Local GTFS, OpenStreetMap, permits. ~1–3 days
  5. Set caps; deploy the container. ~½ day
  6. Train reviewers. ~1 h

Estimates. Text PDFs and public GTFS need no code changes.

Who would use it

  • Residents, no account: renters, owners, riders, seniors, students, parents, people with a disability, newcomers.
  • Clerks: review every statement before publishing.
  • Planners: open-data lenses; ION Stage 2 construction scout.
  • Councillors, groups: one cited link with the dates to take part.

Privacy and the law

  • No personal data by design. No accounts; only public documents go to the model. IPs stay in memory for rate limits, never logged. A logged question keeps only its text, audiences and language.
  • Retention. Staff can purge an entry's content under the retention by-law. The chain still verifies.
  • MFIPPA. A municipality may collect personal information only if a statute authorizes it, for law enforcement, or if necessary to a lawfully authorized activity (s. 28(2)).4
  • Data residency. The Gemini API is a US service; paid-tier content isn't used to improve Google's products.5 Production: Vertex AI in Canada6 or on-premises.
  • Bill 194. Ontario's Enhancing Digital Security and Trust Act, 2024 lets the province require prescribed public bodies using AI to inform the public, keep an accountability framework, manage risk and ensure human oversight.1,2 No regulation yet covers municipal AI.3

What residents should know

  • AI drafts; a named staff member approves each statement.
  • Not the official notice: each page links to it, and it prevails.
  • Every number links to its page. The AI can be wrong.
  • Questions are public; the chat warns you to leave out personal details.
  • Translations are labelled machine-made, not staff-reviewed.
  • "Report an error" goes to the site's operator: here, our GitHub issues; a city would use its own channel.

What's next

Pilot one live decision with a Waterloo Region municipality. Measure review time and understanding with screen-reader users and newcomers. Before real use: a WCAG 2.2 AA audit, a privacy impact assessment, a Canadian-hosted model, billing alerts, OCR.
